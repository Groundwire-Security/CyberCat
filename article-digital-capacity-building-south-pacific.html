Digital capacity building in the South Pacific: practical skills for real businesses

By Sangeeta Devi Deo · 19 November 2026 · 4 minute read

Running a business takes more than a good product or service. Customers need to find you, contact you, trust you and pay you. For most small businesses in the Pacific, that now means websites, email, digital records, online payments and social media.

Where do you start when time, money and technical support are limited?

What is digital capacity building?

Capacity building is a familiar term for development partners, trade organisations and funders. It means strengthening the skills, systems and confidence people need to grow and sustain their work.

Digital capacity building applies that to technology. It is not about turning business owners into IT specialists. It is about practical decisions they can act on the next day.

Practical digital skills for real businesses

Our workshops help business owners and teams work out which tools could make their work easier, more productive and safer. Topics can include:

  • business websites and professional email
  • organising files, records and customer information
  • everyday digital tools and useful ways to use AI
  • recognising scams and protecting business information
  • deciding what to improve next

The focus is on useful decisions, not technology for its own sake. See our workshop topics.

Working with Pacific women in business

In November 2026, CyberCat delivered “Building a Business in a Digital World” at the International Trade Centre’s Pacific Women in Export Workshop in Nukuʻalofa, Tonga. Approximately 50 women business owners and senior executives attended from Fiji, Papua New Guinea, Samoa, Solomon Islands, Tonga and Vanuatu.

Sangeeta Devi and James Bourne facilitated practical activities. Participants reviewed business websites from a customer’s point of view, discussed digital records and online tools, and worked through realistic scam scenarios.

Designed around the businesses in the room

Some businesses need a professional email address or better records. Others are already exporting and want to look at more advanced systems or AI tools. We do not recommend one system for everyone. Workshops help participants choose useful next steps for their own operations.

Digital safety is part of business development

A business that improves its website, sells online or introduces new tools also needs to protect customer information and recognise suspicious requests. That is why cybersecurity awareness is built into every workshop, not added on at the end.

PAWS gives people a simple response to any questionable request: Pause, Assess, Walk away, Separately verify.

Working with development and trade organisations

CyberCat works with organisations that support business development, digital inclusion and entrepreneurship across Australia and the South Pacific. Sessions can be built around an industry, a network or a participant group.
